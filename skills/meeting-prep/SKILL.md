# Meeting prep

You help someone walk into a meeting ready, in the time they have. The output is one prep card they can read in about a minute, not a report.

## Pick the mode (don't ask)

- **Quick**: the call is within about 30 minutes, or the user sounds rushed. Ask at most 2 questions, then write the card.
- **Standard**: an hour or more before the call. Ask up to 4 questions, then write the card.
- **Deep**: the user asks for deep prep, or the stakes are clearly high (a pitch, a first meeting with someone senior, a deal, a reconnect after a long gap). Do the research in "Deep mode" below first.
- **Under 5 minutes**: write only the one-sentence goal, the 3 best questions, the one thing to avoid, and the opener.

## 1. Gather what's already known

Before asking the user anything, look for what you can read yourself:

- If email is connected, search for recent threads with the person (last 6 months). Note what they said last time, anything the user promised them, and anything they promised the user. Quote their words; don't paraphrase them.
- If a calendar is connected, read the invite: who's coming, the title, any agenda or attachments.
- If the user pasted notes, a thread or a LinkedIn profile, use those.
- If you can browse, look at the person's company site and any news from the last 3 months.

Tell the user in one or two lines what you found, so they don't repeat it. Never invent a past conversation, a promise or a fact about the person. If something is unclear, ask.

**A connection date is not a last-contact date.** Don't call it a "reconnect after 10 years" because a profile says they connected in 2015. Ask how well the user knows them.

**A website or profile can be a year behind.** If what you read and what the user says disagree, believe the user and the most recent email.

## 2. Ask only what you can't find

Ask in one message, never one question at a time. The usual set:

- What kind of meeting is it? (customer, pitch, advisor, new contact, reconnect, partner, hiring, interview)
- What would a good outcome be? What do you want to walk out with?
- What do you think they want from it?
- Anything I should know: history, sensitivities, open threads?

Skip any question the context already answers.

## 3. Write the prep card

Use this shape. Bullets, not paragraphs. Keep it under about 250 words for Quick and Standard.

```
# [Their name, exactly as they write it] - [meeting type] - [date/time]

## Opener (read this aloud, then ask your first question)
1. [What you do now, in one plain sentence a stranger could picture]
2. [One concrete proof from the last month]
3. [What you want from this call]

## The one thing
[One sentence: what you want to walk out with]
- Win: [a concrete outcome, like a next meeting booked or an intro promised]
- Lose: [what a wasted call looks like, usually "let's stay in touch"]

## What they want
- [2-3 things they care about right now, from what you read]

## What you can give them
- [1-3 specific things: an intro to a named person, something you read that fits their problem, an honest read, a lead]

## Your ask
- Ask: [one specific thing]
- Fallback: [something smaller if the ask is a stretch]

## Questions
- [3-5 questions, for this meeting type, see the table below]

## Avoid
- [1-3 topics or claims to stay away from]

## Close
"[A closing sentence that names the next step, who owes what, and uses their name]"

## Open threads
- [Anything either side promised last time, quoted]
```

## Questions by meeting type

| Type | Approach | Questions that work |
|---|---|---|
| Customer (discovery) | Ask before you tell. Ask about what they did, not what they'd do | "Tell me about the last time this happened." "What did you do instead?" "What did that cost you?" |
| Customer (pitch) | Tie it to the problem they described. Keep the first step small | "If this worked, what would it unblock?" "Who else needs to be in this?" |
| Advisor | Bring the question already framed, with your options | "If you were me, what would worry you most?" "What am I not seeing?" |
| New contact or intro | Curiosity first. Give something before you ask | "What are you spending most of your time on right now?" "Who in your world should I know?" |
| Reconnect | Read their world first. Bring one new thing they'd care about | "What's changed since we last talked?" "I saw [specific thing], how's that going?" |
| Partner | Their goals first, then the smallest test | "What would the simplest first test look like?" "What would make this fail?" |
| Hiring | Their reasons, not yours | "What are you optimizing for next?" "What would have to be true for you to move?" |
| User interview | Past behavior, never future intent. Don't pitch | "Walk me through the last time you did this." "What did you try before?" |

## Rules that come from real calls

- **Give before you ask.** Always fill in "What you can give them" with something specific. It's usually the part people remember.
- **The opener is 3 sentences, written out, read aloud.** Unwritten, a "quick intro" runs 6 or 7 minutes. Three sentences, then ask the first question and let them talk.
- **Their name goes on the first and last line of the card.** It's easy to say the wrong name at the end of a day of calls.
- **Specific asks beat standing ones.** "Keep me in mind" gets politeness. "Could you introduce me to Sam?" gets a yes or a no.
- **The middle of the call belongs to what's on their mind,** not to your list of questions. Spread the questions out; don't fire them in a row.
- **End on one next step,** said out loud, with who owes what.

## Deep mode

Do this before writing the card when the stakes are high. Cite a source for every claim.

**The person**
- Confirm you have the right person (two signals: name plus company, title or location).
- Career arc: the moves that matter, and what they suggest about what this person values.
- What they've said in public: posts, talks, interviews, podcasts.
- What's changed in the last 3 months: a new role, a launch, a raise, a post that got attention.

**Their company**
- What it does, roughly how big, who it serves.
- Recent news: launches, funding, leadership changes, layoffs.
- Anything that changes what you'd ask or offer.

Then add to the card:
- **What I found** (5-8 bullets with sources)
- **What it means for this call** (2-3 bullets: what to lean into, what to avoid)

Stay on the professional record. Don't dig into family or personal life.

## After the call

End the card with one line: "After the call, tell me what happened and I'll write the follow-up and the next steps."
